# Macedonian (`mk`): needs, conventions and items to check

All content is `candidate`; nothing is verified. Items marked **[CHECK]** are where a native-speaker or linguist reviewer should look first.

How it was checked: the four realizations, the English locale additions and the bridge were overlaid on a fresh scratch copy of the current `gef-lexicon` tree (including the live `ru`, `uk`, `ja`, `ko`, `hi`, `fa`, `pl`, `tr` drops), and `scripts/validate-curriculum-v2.mjs` passed with `mk` as `completeness: "complete"`, `slots_version: 2` (every slot, example, group, label and gloss present). In the copy, removing a label, a gloss and a symbol from the groups made it fail, so the check does reach `mk`. **The copy needed two vocabulary additions to `slots.json` (section 1); without them the real validator will fail on `mk`.** The real tree was not touched. I read the app's intro code (`introScript.ts`, `introSound.ts`, `introGrammar.ts`) to see how the facts are used; where that changed a choice it is said below.

Sources: en.wikipedia "Macedonian alphabet" (31 letters, the order of ѓ and ќ, the grave letters ѐ ѝ, letter names, IPA), "Macedonian phonology" (five vowels, antepenultimate stress, word-final voicing neutralised, /ɫ/ against /l/ with the pair бела/беља, /x/ only in loans and a few native words, the palatal stops), "Romanization of Macedonian" (the digraph system used on passports since 2008, `j` for ј), "Macedonian grammar" (no noun cases except traces of the vocative, the postposed article in three forms, adjectives before the noun, SVO as canonical order, particles `ли` and `дали`), "Macedonian language" (about 1.7 million native speakers, North Macedonia and the diaspora, the clitic sentence "Јас не му ја дадов книгата на момчето", the renarrative mood), "Inferential mood" (Macedonian "Тој бил богат!" and the Bulgarian pair "той отиде / отишъл" as the closest parallel), "Clitic doubling" (obligatory with definite direct and indirect objects in standard Macedonian); mk.wikipedia "Македонска азбука" (ѐ and ѝ are homograph markers, not letters; it gives no letter names). en.wiktionary, read through the MediaWiki API: the `{{mk-IPA}}` output for the IPA of every example word, the Macedonian entries for every gloss and gender (including that `нива` is the Macedonian word for field, so the Serbian `њива` is NOT used), the letter entries for the letter names, and the Proto-Slavic etymologies for the cognates.

## 1. Vocabulary and labels

**Two vocabulary additions are needed in `curriculum-v2/slots/slots.json`** (the brief allows this; both are in the scratch copy):
- `writing.mark_roles` gets **`homograph`**, for the grave accent of `ѐ ѝ`. None of the existing roles fits: the accent changes neither sound nor stress, it only tells two words spelled alike apart (ѝ "to her" from и "and"; сѐ "everything" from се). Fallback if the lead prefers no new role: use `stress` and drop the `markrole.homograph` label, but then the lesson would say "Shows which syllable is stressed", which is untrue here.
- `sound.stress_patterns` gets **`antepenultimate`**. Macedonian stress is a fixed rule: the third syllable from the end, the first in a word of two. `mostly_penultimate` and `unpredictable` are both wrong, and `rule_with_written_accents` says accents are written. Label `stress.antepenultimate` is written in `locale-en.json` (the brief says not to write generic `stress.*` labels because they exist; this one is new).

**New ids used in the data:** layer id `cyrillic` (as ru and uk); branch ids `slavic`, `south_slavic` (in that order, so "Where it comes from" says "South Slavic"); sound variety `standard_macedonian`; ancestor tag `chu` (Old Church Slavonic, ISO 639-3; drop it if the app resolves ancestor tags against the registry, it is not in it).

**New labels written in `locale-en.json`:**
- language_overview: `branch.south_slavic`
- writing_system: `markrole.homograph`
- sound_system: `stress.antepenultimate`, `sound.mk_kj`, `sound.mk_gj`, `sound.mk_dz`, `sound.mk_lj`, `sound.mk_nj`, `sound.mk_l_pair`
- grammar_glance: none (every trait value label already exists)

**Existing labels reused, so not rewritten:** `family.indo_european`, `branch.slavic`, `region.europe`, `script.Cyrl`, `layer.cyrillic`, `layer.cyrillic.lesson` ("Learn the Cyrillic alphabet"), `layer.cyrillic.note`, `mark.grave`, `sound.trill` (for р), `sound.velar_fricative` (for х, and the храна/рана pair), all `trait.*` value labels, `gender.*`, `register.*`.

**[CHECK] `layer.cyrillic.note`** ("Everyday writing leaves out stress marks. Learner books put them back.") is shown for Macedonian too (shared layer id). It is true that ordinary text has no stress marks; "learner books put them back" is not really a Macedonian habit, because the stress is predictable. Harmless, but the lead may want it softened for all Cyrillic languages.

**Labels to confirm:** `sound.mk_kj` / `sound.mk_gj` compare ќ and ѓ to "ty" and "dy" as in the British "tune" and "dune". The real sounds are palatal stops [c] and [ɟ] (they are [tɕ dʑ] in some dialects and merged with ч and џ in the town speech of Prilep), so "ty / dy" is an approximation. `sound.mk_lj` and `sound.mk_nj` repeat the wording of the existing `palatal_lateral` and `palatal_nasal` labels and add the shape hint ("like л / н with a small tail"), which is why they are new ids and not reuses. `sound.mk_l_pair` says "Before a, o or u, л is a dark l", because before e, i and j the л is light (Wikipedia: only /l/ occurs there).

## 2. Conventions I chose (please confirm or change)

**Letters and groups**
- `symbols`: the 31 letters of the 1945 alphabet in alphabetical order, lowercase: `а б в г д ѓ е ж з ѕ и ј к л љ м н њ о п р с т ќ у ф х ц ч џ ш`. `ѓ` follows `д` and `ќ` follows `т` (Wikipedia: they are ordered by sound, not by shape). `ѐ` and `ѝ` are not letters: `marked: ["ѐ","ѝ"]`, with no example of their own (precomposed U+0450 and U+045D). They appear in the lesson through the one `grave` mark fact: value `ѐ`, example `сѐ` ("everything"); the gloss also names ѝ and its contrast with и.
- Nine teaching groups of 3 to 5, easy-first, following the brief: (1) `а е о` and (2) `к м т` look and sound like Latin; (3) `в н р` and (4) `с у х` are the look-alikes that sound different (`looks_like` B H P, C y X); (5) `б д л п` and (6) `з ф г и` are new shapes with mostly familiar sounds; (7) `ж ч ш ц` the hushing and hissing sounds; then the Macedonian-specific letters last, as asked: (8) `ј љ њ` (the y-sound and the two soft consonants) and (9) `ѓ ќ ѕ џ` (the palatal stops and the two affricates). For a Russian, Ukrainian or Bulgarian reader the short lesson is therefore exactly groups 8 and 9 (plus the quotation mark). In every group no example word contains another letter of the same group, so "which letter is in the word you hear?" never has two right answers (checked by script).
- `looks_like` is set on в (B), н (H), р (P), с (C), у (y), х (X), п (n), **ј (j)** and **ѕ (S)**. ј is the strongest trap (it looks like the Latin j and sounds like English y); ѕ looks like S and sounds "dz". Not set for љ and њ: no Latin letter resembles them; the "л / н with a small tail" hint is in their sound labels (`sound.mk_lj`, `sound.mk_nj`).
- **`reading` scheme [CHECK].** The digraph system used on Macedonian passports since 2008 (ICAO 9303: `zh dz gj kj lj nj sh ch`), with **three deliberate departures** for an English reader: `ј` is **`y`** (official `j`, which an English reader would say as in "jam"; the tile also says "Looks like j" and /j/), `х` is **`kh`** (official `h`, which would read as the English h; the same as the Russian and Ukrainian drops), `ц` is **`ts`** (official `c`; the Romanization article notes `ts` is also used). `џ` is `dzh` (the article says `dj` or `dzh`). All 31 readings are different (the app's match drill and sound question break on duplicates).
- **`name` [CHECK].** Following the en.wiktionary pronunciation sections for each letter: a vowel letter is called by its sound (а, е, и, о, у), a consonant letter by its sound plus a schwa when spelled aloud (en.wikipedia "Macedonian phonology": "When spelling aloud, each consonant is followed by the schwa"), written `б’ (bə)` with an apostrophe for the schwa, as Macedonian writes a schwa. en.wikipedia's alphabet table gives English-style names (Be, Ve, Ge, Gje, Dze, Kje, ...) and mk.wikipedia gives none, so there is no printed standard; the `name` qualifiers are all optional and can be deleted without breaking anything (the app then simply shows no name).
- `sound` is the letter's own main sound, broad IPA: `a ɛ i ɔ u` for the vowels, `ɡ` (U+0261), `ɟ` (ѓ), `c` (ќ), `dz` (ѕ), `ʎ` (љ), `ɲ` (њ), `ɫ` (л), `x` (х), `ts tʃ dʒ` without tie bars. **[CHECK] `л` is `ɫ`:** Wikipedia lists /ɫ/ and /l/ for л and /ʎ/ for љ in the alphabet article, but its phonology article treats the sound written љ as a plain alveolar /l/ that contrasts with /ɫ/ (бела [ˈbɛɫa], беља [ˈbɛʎa] in Wiktionary). Wiktionary writes љ as /ʎ~lʲ/. I used `ʎ` for љ and `ɫ` for л, and the example for л (`лав`) has a back vowel so the dark sound is the one the learner hears.
- **Example words** are concrete, short (7 letters at most; `јаболко` is the longest), plain (no stress marks) and native where a native word is as common. Facts worth knowing: `ѕвезда` (star) and `заб` (tooth) are the Macedonian words (звезда and зуб are Serbian or Bulgarian); `нива` is field, so for њ I used `коњ` (horse), which ENDS in њ (almost no everyday Macedonian word starts with њ); љ is rare in Macedonian, `љубов` (love) is the best common word and is abstract; `лут` (angry, spicy) is written without љ. **Loans that were the best picture-able choice for their letter [CHECK]:** `ананас`, `икона` (almost every word starting with и is a loan or abstract), `фустан`, `џеб`, `ѓубре` ("garbage"), `шише`. `тато` is the informal "dad" (the standard word is `татко`); `елен` (deer) was chosen over `еж` (hedgehog) because `еж` is said [ɛʃ] (final voicing neutralised) and would show ж sounding like ш in the first group; `лав` (lion) is said [ɫaf] for the same reason, which is fine for a letter that is not ж. `ќерка` (daughter) and `ѓубре` start with their letters.
- Stress is shown in the IPA only (antepenult rule; one-syllable words carry no stress mark).

**Punctuation** `„` [CHECK]: the opening low double quotation mark (U+201E), listed because an English reader does not have it. The example is `„Здраво!“` ("quotes"; no `sound`, as the validator exempts punctuation; name `наводници`, Wiktionary has `наводник` "quotation mark"). I could not find a source that says in plain words that `„ “` is the primary Macedonian quotation style (en.wikipedia "Macedonian grammar" lists `„ “` among the marks of the orthography); the Macedonian orthography (Правопис, 2017) is the source to check. If it is wrong or unwanted, set `punctuation` to `[]` and delete the `quotes` example.

**Marks**
- Only one: `grave`, role `homograph` (see section 1), `writing.cyrillic.mark.grave`. The grave is used in the standard orthography only to tell homographs apart (`ѝ`/`и`, `сѐ`/`се`); ordinary writing otherwise has no accents, and the stress is not written. A reader who already knows the grave accent (French) skips it.

**Sounds**
- **IPA is broad, adapted from Wiktionary's `{{mk-IPA}}`**: final voicing neutralisation applied (заб [zap], лав [ɫaf], нов [nɔf], џеб [dʒɛp], ѕид [dzit]; Wikipedia's phonology notes it is not written), stress by the antepenult rule, `ɫ` for the dark л, tie bars removed, and the intervocalic tapped `ɾ` Wiktionary shows in some words written `r` throughout (the Russian and Ukrainian drops use `r`). **[CHECK]** whether a phonemic transcription (no devoicing, `ɫaʋ`) would suit the app's text-to-speech better; I kept the surface forms because they are what the learner should hear.
- `sounds.vowels`: `i ɛ a ɔ u` (five; no reduction, vowel length not phonemic). `sounds.consonants`: 27 symbols including the palatal pair `c ɟ`, `ɲ`, `ʎ`, both laterals `l ɫ`, the affricates `ts dz tʃ dʒ`, and `x`. **[CHECK]** `x` is not native to the language (Wikipedia: loans, place names, church words, and a few disambiguations such as храна/рана), and the schwa (written with an apostrophe, as in `'рж`) is marginal in the standard, so the schwa is not listed among the vowels.
- Seven `sounds.tricky.*`: ќ (ќебе), ѓ (меѓу), ѕ (ѕид), љ (љубов), њ (коњ), the trilled р (рака, `sound.trill` reused) and х (хотел, `sound.velar_fricative` reused). No tricky word contains another tricky spelling, because the app's "which spelling makes the sound in this word?" question offers all seven spellings as options (checked by script). All seven sounds are missing from the English inventory (`c ɟ dz ʎ ɲ r x`), so each is tagged "New to you" for an English reader.
- Four contrast pairs, all real and common (glosses from Wiktionary): бела/беља (dark л or soft љ; white (f.) or trouble), коњ/кон (њ or н; horse or "towards"), храна/рана (х or no х; food or wound), куќа/кука (ќ or к; house or hook). **Deliberately not drilled:** ѓ against г, and ѕ against з or ц, because I found no pair I could verify. **[CHECK]** the pairs with a native speaker, especially that бела is the feminine form of бел (it has no entry of its own on Wiktionary).
- `stress.pattern: antepenultimate` (new value). `spelling.predictability: high`: the orthography is phonemic (about one letter per sound, no silent letters), final devoicing is not written, and the stress is regular; `high` is not controversial. `tones.system: none`.
- **No vowel reduction and no case** are not facts the slots can carry; they are visible only through `sounds.vowels` (five clear vowels) and `has_case: true` (capital letters exist). I did not make "unstressed vowels stay clear" a tricky sound because its spelling (о, е...) would appear inside the other tricky words.

**Grammar** (all twelve traits have a value; the closest value is chosen where the fit is imperfect)
- **`articles: definite_only` [CHECK, imperfect fit].** Macedonian has a definite article that is a suffix, `-от -та -то -те` (neutral), with `-ов -ва -во -ве` (near) and `-он -на -но -не` (far) as variants; the examples show `книга / книгата`, `стол / столот`, `дете / детето`. The generic label says "has a word for “the” but none for “a”", and the Macedonian "the" is an ending, not a word. There is no indefinite article in the standard grammars, though `еден / една / едно` ("one") is used a little like "a" in speech.
- **`noun_gender: three`; `articles` qualifier for the gender check [CHECK, imperfect fit].** The app's gender question ("Which little word goes with …?") needs a word that goes before the noun, and Macedonian "the" goes after it, so I used the possessive `мој / моја / мое` ("my"), as the Russian and Ukrainian drops do. Six nouns, two of each gender: стол, град (m), книга, куќа (f), дете, село (n). A reviewer who prefers the demonstratives `овој / оваа / ова` can swap them.
- **`cases: pronouns_only` [CHECK].** The user's note is "no noun cases (lost)"; that is the value. Pronouns keep subject, object and indirect-object forms (`јас / ме / мене`), and a few nouns keep a vocative (`пријателе`, `душо`), which the label glosses over ("only pronouns change"). The third and fourth examples are the **clitic doubling**: `Ја видов Ана` and `Му ја дадов книгата на момчето`, the second a simplified affirmative form of the sentence quoted in en.wikipedia "Macedonian language"; clitic doubling is obligatory with definite objects in the standard language (en.wikipedia "Clitic doubling"). There is no slot for clitic doubling, so it lives under `cases` (it is the object-pronoun system in use).
- **`verb_endings: many`, example verb `зборувам`** (verified a-verb paradigm: зборувам, зборуваш, зборува, зборуваме, зборувате, зборуваат). Three examples: the present paradigm; past, present and future (`зборував / зборувам / ќе зборувам`); and the **renarrative mood** (`Тој дојде. / Тој дошол.`, "He came (I know it) / He came (so they say)"), because there is **no evidentiality trait** in the slots. The aorist form `дојде` against the l-participle `дошол` is the Macedonian analogue of Bulgarian "той отиде / отишъл" (en.wikipedia "Inferential mood"); **[CHECK] the exact sentence and gloss with a native speaker.** I did not use "Тој бил богат!" (the only Macedonian sentence on Wikipedia) because it is the admirative use ("he is rich! (surprise)").
- `word_order: svo` as the user asked. **[CHECK]** Standard Macedonian is SVO in neutral order but quite free (the clitics show who does what), so `flexible` is defensible and would read "word order is flexible, because endings show who does what", which is not quite right here either; I kept `svo` and added a second example with the object first (`Книгата ја чита мама`).
- `subject_pronouns: often_dropped`, example `Зборувам македонски.` (verb endings show the person; the past tenses with `сум` are the same). `questions: particle` (`дали` at the start, `ли` after the verb; both shown; a statement with rising voice also works). `formal_you: distinct`, `informal: ти`, `formal: Вие` (a capital В in polite writing); the two examples differ in the verb (`зборуваш` / `зборувате`). `adjective_position: before_noun` (`голем стол`, `голема куќа`; the article goes on the adjective, `големиот стол`). `word_building: inflecting` (`зборуваме`, `сум читал / сум читала`, gender-marked past participle). `classifiers: none`. `dual_number: none` (the counted form `два стола` is not a dual).

**Overview**
- `family_id: indo_european`, `branch_ids: [slavic, south_slavic]`. Because the app treats any shared branch id as "same branch", a Russian or Ukrainian reader sees Macedonian as a "close cousin" (shared `slavic`), and a Bulgarian, Serbian or Slovene reader (once they list `south_slavic`) as the closest. `related_language_tags`: `bg`, `sr`, `bs`, `hr`, `sl` (South Slavic) then `ru`, `uk`, `pl` (a sample of the farther Slavic cousins); all are in the registry. `bg` first because it is the closest relative; the notes say it is largely understood by speakers of Macedonian and vice versa, wording chosen to stay neutral (Wikipedia: most linguists outside Bulgaria treat Macedonian as a language of its own).
- `reach.regions: [europe]` and `origin_region_ids: [europe]`. The diaspora (Australia, Canada, the United States) is large but not listed, as with the Ukrainian drop. **[CHECK]** whether the app wants `americas` and `oceania` here.
- Hook: `hook.target` `здраво` (hi, hello; informal), decoys `благодарам` (thank you) and `да` (yes). They carry `reading` (`zdravo`, `blagodaram`, `da`) as the Russian and Ukrainian drops do. The formal greeting `добар ден` was not used (longer, harder to hear as a first word).

## 3. Bridge (`bridge-en-mk.json`)

- English and Macedonian are distant cousins and English has almost no words from Macedonian, so the bridge is **mostly true cognates**: **one `helpful_transfer` note** (the app shows only the first note's text as the page text), 17 pairs: 15 old shared words, then two words of Slavic origin. The note text says honestly which is which.
- **Cognates (15):** brother/брат, sister/сестра, mother/мајка, two/два, three/три, ten/десет, night/ноќ, new/нов, water/вода, name/име, nose/нос, salt/сол, wolf/волк, goose/гуска, beard/брада. Checked in the en.wiktionary Macedonian entries and the Proto-Slavic reconstruction pages: брат (*bratrъ from PIE *bʰréh₂tēr), сестра (*swésōr), три (*tréyes), два (*dwóh₁), ноќ (*nókʷts), нов (*néwos), вода (*wódr̥), име (*h₁nómn̥), сол (*sḗh₂l), волк (*wĺ̥kʷos), гуска (*ǵʰh₂éns), брада (*bʰardʰéh₂), десет (*déḱm̥t), мајка (the Macedonian entry derives it from Proto-Slavic *mati, from the same PIE word as "mother"; **[CHECK]** that the `-jka` form still counts as the same word, `мати` is the older Slavic form), нос (from Proto-Slavic *nosъ). **[CHECK]** the English side (PIE roots of night, new, nose, beard, goose, ten) from standard Indo-European etymology; I read them only on the Slavic side.
- **Left out:** `млеко` (milk). It looks like a cognate, but the Proto-Slavic page says *melko was borrowed from Gothic or West Germanic, so it is not an inherited relative. `ќерка` (daughter, a true cognate of "daughter" but unrecognisable), `мед` (honey, cognate of "mead" but the meaning differs), `син` (it means "blue" too), `ти` (thou).
- **Slavic words English has (2):** vampire/вампир and robot/робот. en.wiktionary: Macedonian `вампир` is inherited from Proto-Slavic *ǫpyrь, English "vampire" came from a Slavic word by way of German and French (**[CHECK]** the route); Macedonian `робот` is borrowed from Czech, and English "robot" is also from Czech, so the two words look alike for the same reason.
- **One `false_friend` note, 5 pairs** (meanings from Wiktionary): magazine/магазин (a warehouse or storeroom; the Macedonian for the periodical is `списание`), fabric/фабрика (a factory), son/сон (a dream, sleep; son is `син`), list/лист (a leaf, a sheet; a list is `список`), red/ред (a row, a line, a turn; red is `црвен`). The last three look alike only once the Cyrillic is sounded out. **[CHECK]** the meanings with a native speaker. Left out as not true false friends (Wiktionary gives the English sense too): `актуелен` (also "actual"), `кабинет` (also "cabinet"), `бар` (also "bar").
- Bridge IPA follows the same Wiktionary-derived convention as the realizations.
