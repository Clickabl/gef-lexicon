# Agent prompt — Being grammar research

Use `research/grammar/being/README.md` as the universal contract and the assigned language file as the only output document.

## Instruction

Research how the assigned language expresses the semantic territory commonly covered by English `be`, Spanish `ser/estar/hay`, and related copular/existential/state/location systems.

Do **not** translate another language's rules. Determine this language's own forms, constructions, omissions, morphology, cases, particles, adjective/verb predication, existential strategies, and decision boundaries.

Use authoritative sources where possible: national language academies, university/reference grammars, peer-reviewed linguistic work, or established pedagogical authorities. Record what each source supports. Avoid copying copyrighted dictionary/reference prose; summarize findings in original wording.

Fill the assigned research document with:

1. form/construction inventory;
2. mapping to the universal research slots;
3. structured candidate decision rules;
4. morphosyntax and omission/zero-copula behavior;
5. auxiliary-vs-copular distinctions;
6. real learner traps;
7. Gef corpus examples if available;
8. source/evidence map;
9. unresolved questions and required human/native review.

All output remains `candidate`.

### Tier 1
Research deeply enough to support eventual complete grammar-path coverage for this domain.

### Tier 2
Stop once this specific Being lesson can represent the language accurately. Do not turn the assignment into a complete grammar curriculum.

## Output discipline

Prefer structured facts over prose. Every proposed rule should be reducible to fields such as:

`form_or_construction`, `semantic_slots`, `conditions`, `exclusions`, `contrast_with`, `register_or_variety`, `morphosyntax`, `examples`, `source_refs`, `review_state`.

If the universal slot model is inadequate, propose a new slot and justify it rather than forcing the fact into the nearest English-shaped category.
